A complex origami sculpture does not appear fold by fold, the way a simple model does from a diagram. It is built in **stages**, and each stage has a different job. The photographs below follow one sculpture, *The Monkey King*, from a flat, creased sheet to the finished figure. The step notes are the artist's own.

## 1. Grid and pre-creasing

> After preparing the paper, we make a grid and pre-crease the fold.

![A large white sheet covered in a dense pre-creased grid](/media/process/sun-wukong-step-1.jpg "150 cm white sheet, pre-creased on a 52 grid.")

The sheet is first divided into a regular **grid**, for this sculpture, 52 divisions along each edge of a 150 cm square. The lines of the crease pattern are then folded in advance. Nothing looks like a figure yet: this stage is pure geometry, and it is where most of the precision of the final model is decided.

## 2. Collapse

> Then we collapse high priority structures like scales.

![Part of the creased sheet beginning to collapse](/media/process/sun-wukong-step-2.jpg "Scales and other high-priority structures are collapsed first.")

Collapsing means bringing all the pre-creases together at once so that the flat sheet folds into a three-dimensional base. Areas with the densest, most repetitive structure (here, the scales) are collapsed first.

## 3. Collapse complete

> After following every line in the crease pattern, we start with pre-shaping.

![The fully collapsed base with long flaps radiating from the body](/media/process/sun-wukong-step-3.jpg "The completed base: every flap the figure needs, in the right place.")

At this point the crease pattern has done everything it can. In the artist's words: *“End of collapse ends whatever we can get from a crease pattern. Shaping depends on our own creativity.”* The base contains a flap for every limb, plume and weapon, but none of the character.

## 4. Pre-shaping

> Here we add small details before we start with shaping.

![Close view of a flap covered in small pyramid-shaped scales](/media/process/sun-wukong-step-4.jpg "Detail added before shaping. No MC, glue or super glue is used at this stage.")

Pre-shaping adds the small, crisp details that are easier to form while the base is still relatively flat. No MC (methylcellulose), glue or super glue is used in pre-shaping.

## 5. Shaping

> The hardest and most time-consuming part begins now.

![The figure on the table mid-shaping](/media/process/sun-wukong-step-5.jpg "This is where the transformation happens.")

Shaping turns a geometric base into a sculpture: posture, anatomy, costume and expression. It is slow, judgement-driven work, and it is where two folders working from the same crease pattern will produce very different results.

## 6. Finished fold

> After rigorous shaping, the model is completed.

![The finished Monkey King standing on the studio desk](/media/process/sun-wukong-step-6.jpg "Shaping alone took more than 24 hours.")

For *The Monkey King*, shaping alone took more than 24 hours.

## What to take from this

- **Design and folding are separate problems.** The crease pattern solves *where* every flap is; shaping decides *what it becomes*.
- **Precision is front-loaded.** Errors in the grid travel through every later stage.
- **Large sheets are not a luxury.** A 52 grid on 150 cm gives cells of about 2.9 cm; the same grid on a 50 cm sheet would give cells under 1 cm. See [Grids and sheet size](/learn/grids-and-sheet-size).
